# Doctor Commercial Performance & ROI Tracker

* **Author:** Exponit Labs Core Architecture
* **Status:** Draft
* **Date:** 2026-11-01
* **Target Module:** Admin Console CMS (`/console/doctor-portfolio`)

---

## Overview

The Doctor Commercial Performance & ROI Tracker is an executive decision-support interface embedded inside the Exponit Labs Filament v5 administrative console (`/console`). It calculates and visualizes the net commercial return generated by the company's 233 registered healthcare providers relative to the operational capital invested in maintaining those relationships.

By correlating field representative detailing calls (DCRs), distributed drug samples, and promotional collateral with monthly prescription revenue, the system categorizes physicians into objective commercial performance tiers:
- **High-Yield Prescribers (Net Positive):** Commercial return significantly exceeds invested detailing and sampling capital.
- **Balanced Prescribers:** Prescriptions cover ongoing field force maintenance and sample costs.
- **Under-Yielding Prescribers (Net Negative):** Cumulative detailing expenses cross threshold limits without generating reciprocal prescription volume.

---

## The Problem

Exponit Labs deploys field Medical Representatives (MRs) across target territories, spending substantial capital on daily physical clinic visits, commercial medicine samples, and promotional brand collateral. 

Currently:
1. **Expenses are tracked in a vacuum:** Daily Call Reports (`d_c_r_s`), drug samples (`dcr_products`), and promotional items (`dcr_promotional_inputs`) log activity, but lack cost valuation.
2. **Revenue is disconnected:** Prescription sales flow through distributors and retail chemists without being attributed back to prescribing doctors in the primary operational database.
3. **Resource allocation is intuitive rather than data-driven:** Sales managers cannot identify which high-frequency doctors fail to generate commercial returns, leading to misallocated field rep travel time and wasted sampling budgets.

---

## Goals

1. **Quantifiable Account-Level Investment:** Calculate total capital deployed per doctor by aggregating:
   - Call visit expenses (baseline site-wide cost per call, with optional doctor-level override).
   - Commercial drug samples handed over (quantity $\times$ sample unit cost).
   - Promotional collateral and gifts delivered (quantity $\times$ promotional input unit cost).
2. **Attributed Revenue Ingestion:** Provide an idempotent monthly prescription revenue ingestion mechanism (`doctor_revenues`) supporting fast inline entry and CSV batch uploads in Filament.
3. **Executive Performance Dashboard:** Provide a dedicated Filament v5 management page at `/console/doctor-portfolio` displaying:
   - Portfolio summary metrics (Total Invested Capital, Total Attributed Revenue, Net P&L, and Overall Return Multiple).
   - Filterable, sortable prescriber ledger with color-coded Net P&L (₹) and ROI status tiers.
   - Accounting period filters (All Time, Current Financial Year [Apr–Mar], Trailing 90 Days, Last Closed Month).
4. **SQLite Query Efficiency:** Execute all aggregations in database queries via correlated subqueries and composite indexes, preventing N+1 execution bottlenecks.
5. **Mathematical Robustness:** Guarantee zero division-by-zero crashes when evaluating doctors with zero historical expenses or zero revenue.

---

## Non-Goals

1. **Direct Live ERPNext Database Sync (v1):** Direct MariaDB connections or synchronous API hooks into local Frappe/ERPNext instances are excluded for v1. Revenue data remains native to Laravel's database; automated ERP synchronization is postponed to a v2 background queue importer.
2. **Mobile Field App (`/mr`) Exposure:** Field representatives will not see financial margins, doctor P&L, or return tiers on their mobile devices. Detailing activity entry on `/mr` remains unaffected.
3. **Bespoke Livewire SPA Architecture:** No separate Single Page Application or custom charting ribbons outside Filament. All views utilize standard Filament v5 tables, schemas, and stats widgets.

---

## Open Questions

* **Snapshot vs. Dynamic Historical Accounting:** Should v1 line items record a `unit_cost_snapshot` at the time of DCR logging to guarantee absolute historical immutability, or is standard master catalog pricing acceptable for initial rollout? (Documented in Technical Plan).
* **Doctor Matching on CSV Uploads:** Should batch revenue uploads match doctors strictly by `doctor_id` (database ID) or also support fuzzy matching on `clinic_name` / `name` with a validation preview?

## Critical User Journeys (CUJs)

### CUJ 1: Executive Triages Under-Yielding Accounts
* **Given**: A sales director is reviewing Q2 territory performance in `/console/doctor-portfolio`.
* **When**: The director selects "This Financial Year" and sorts the table by Net P&L ascending.
* **Then**: The top of the table highlights doctors with high call frequency and heavy sample consumption who have generated minimal or zero attributed revenue, allowing management to reduce rep call frequency on those accounts.

### CUJ 2: Marketing Manager Ingests Monthly Chemist Prescription Audit
* **Given**: Monthly secondary sales audit figures have arrived for October 2026.
* **When**: The manager clicks "Upload Monthly Revenue" on the portfolio page, selects the October 2026 period, and uploads a validated CSV file.
* **Then**: The system validates doctor IDs, updates or inserts revenue records idempotently, logs the import batch, and updates portfolio Net P&L across all dashboard cards without duplicating existing records.

### CUJ 3: Admin Sets Base Detailing and Promotional Input Costs
* **Given**: The price of promotional kits has changed and average visit travel allowances have been revised.
* **When**: The admin updates the unit cost on `PromotionalInputResource` and adjusts the default visit cost setting.
* **Then**: New DCR activities reflect the updated cost baselines immediately in future calculations.

### 3. Financial Calculation Formulations

For any given doctor $d$ and selected date window $[T_{\text{start}}, T_{\text{end}}]$:

$$\text{Effective Visit Cost} = d.\text{visit\_cost\_override} \mathbin{??} \text{config}('exponit.default\_visit\_cost', 250.00)$$

$$\text{Visit Investment} = \text{Count}(\text{DCRs in } [T_{\text{start}}, T_{\text{end}}]) \times \text{Effective Visit Cost}$$

$$\text{Sample Investment} = \sum_{\text{dcr\_products}} (\text{quantity} \times \text{products.sample\_cost})$$

$$\text{Promotional Investment} = \sum_{\text{dcr\_promotional\_inputs}} (\text{quantity} \times \text{promotional\_inputs.unit\_cost})$$

$$\text{Total Invested Capital} = \text{Visit Investment} + \text{Sample Investment} + \text{Promotional Investment}$$

$$\text{Total Attributed Revenue} = \sum_{\text{doctor\_revenues in } [T_{\text{start}}, T_{\text{end}}]} \text{amount}$$

$$\text{Net Commercial P\&L} = \text{Total Attributed Revenue} - \text{Total Invested Capital}$$

$$\text{ROI \%} = \begin{cases} 
\text{null / "Organic"} & \text{if } \text{Total Invested Capital} = 0 \text{ and Revenue} > 0 \\
0.0\% & \text{if } \text{Total Invested Capital} = 0 \text{ and Revenue} = 0 \\
-100.0\% & \text{if } \text{Total Invested Capital} > 0 \text{ and Revenue} = 0 \\
\frac{\text{Net Commercial P\&L}}{\text{Total Invested Capital}} \times 100 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

### 4. High-Performance SQLite Query Service

To prevent N+1 query execution and permit native sorting on computed metrics in Filament, all aggregations are executed through a dedicated service: `App\Services\DoctorPortfolioQueryService`.

```php
namespace App\Services;

use App\Models\Doctor;
use Illuminate\Database\Eloquent\Builder;
use Illuminate\Support\Carbon;

class DoctorPortfolioQueryService
{
    public function getBaseQuery(?Carbon $startDate = null, ?Carbon $endDate = null): Builder
    {
        $defaultVisitCost = config('exponit.default_visit_cost', 250.00);

        return Doctor::query()
            ->with(['area.headquarter'])
            ->select('doctors.*')
            ->selectSub(function ($query) use ($startDate, $endDate) {
                $query->from('d_c_r_s')
                    ->whereColumn('d_c_r_s.doctor_id', 'doctors.id')
                    ->when($startDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('d_c_r_s.date', '>=', $startDate))
                    ->when($endDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('d_c_r_s.date', '<=', $endDate))
                    ->selectRaw('COUNT(*)');
            }, 'visits_count')
            ->selectSub(function ($query) use ($startDate, $endDate) {
                $query->from('dcr_products')
                    ->join('d_c_r_s', 'd_c_r_s.id', '=', 'dcr_products.dcr_id')
                    ->join('products', 'products.id', '=', 'dcr_products.product_id')
                    ->whereColumn('d_c_r_s.doctor_id', 'doctors.id')
                    ->when($startDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('d_c_r_s.date', '>=', $startDate))
                    ->when($endDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('d_c_r_s.date', '<=', $endDate))
                    ->selectRaw('COALESCE(SUM(dcr_products.quantity * products.sample_cost), 0)');
            }, 'samples_cost')
            ->selectSub(function ($query) use ($startDate, $endDate) {
                $query->from('dcr_promotional_inputs')
                    ->join('d_c_r_s', 'd_c_r_s.id', '=', 'dcr_promotional_inputs.dcr_id')
                    ->join('promotional_inputs', 'promotional_inputs.id', '=', 'dcr_promotional_inputs.promotional_input_id')
                    ->whereColumn('d_c_r_s.doctor_id', 'doctors.id')
                    ->when($startDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('d_c_r_s.date', '>=', $startDate))
                    ->when($endDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('d_c_r_s.date', '<=', $endDate))
                    ->selectRaw('COALESCE(SUM(dcr_promotional_inputs.quantity * promotional_inputs.unit_cost), 0)');
            }, 'promos_cost')
            ->selectSub(function ($query) use ($startDate, $endDate) {
                $query->from('doctor_revenues')
                    ->whereColumn('doctor_revenues.doctor_id', 'doctors.id')
                    ->when($startDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('doctor_revenues.period_date', '>=', $startDate))
                    ->when($endDate, fn ($q) => $q->where('doctor_revenues.period_date', '<=', $endDate))
                    ->selectRaw('COALESCE(SUM(doctor_revenues.amount), 0)');
            }, 'total_revenue');
    }
}
```

This structure ensures that Filament table columns for `visits_count`, `samples_cost`, `promos_cost`, `total_revenue`, and derived SQL columns for `net_pnl` can be sorted natively with SQL `ORDER BY` and zero memory degradation.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative A: Direct ERPNext Database Connection (MariaDB Socket Link)
* **Pros**: Direct access to raw sales invoice entries stored in the local Frappe ERPNext system without requiring separate manual uploads.
* **Cons**:
  1. *Severe Cross-Stack Coupling:* Tying Laravel's operational console to an external local MariaDB instance creates brittle multi-stack dependencies (Python/Bench vs. PHP/Herd). If the MariaDB service is halted, `/console` encounters fatal PDO connection errors.
  2. *Domain Mismatch (Stockists vs. Doctors):* In pharmaceutical ERPs, sales invoices are issued to commercial distributors, stockists, and retail chemists—not prescribing doctors. ERPNext does not inherently track which box of medicine was prescribed by which physician without a custom prescriber-line tracking system.
* **Reason for Rejection**: Fails to resolve doctor attribution and introduces severe cross-system operational fragility. Decoupling revenue into a native SQLite table with a future v2 asynchronous API importer is significantly more robust.

### Alternative B: Standalone SPA Frontend with Broker-Terminal Styling
* **Pros**: Complete aesthetic freedom to build custom high-density stock-broker terminal layouts, trading ribbons, and ticker animations.
* **Cons**:
  1. *Infrastructure Duplication:* Requires duplicate authentication, routing, and deployment infrastructure outside the established `/console` Filament CMS.
  2. *Ethical & Regulatory Risks:* Framing physicians with Wall Street stock tickers, "gainers vs. losers", and "capital drains" introduces cognitive friction and violates pharmaceutical marketing compliance norms (e.g., UCPMP in India) if reports are shared or audited.
  3. *Maintenance Overhead:* Diverges from Filament v5's design system and breaks native accessibility and responsive table controls.
* **Reason for Rejection**: Native Filament v5 components (`StatsOverviewWidget`, Table filters, and badges) satisfy 100% of executive business requirements with zero redundant framework overhead.

### Alternative C: Dynamic Eloquent Model Accessors (`$doctor->total_investment`)
* **Pros**: Rapid to write using standard Laravel Eloquent relationship helpers.
* **Cons**:
  1. *N+1 Query Explosion:* Calculating totals in PHP accessors requires executing 4 queries per doctor row. At 50 doctors per page, that is 201 database roundtrips per request.
  2. *Pagination & Sorting Failure:* Filament Table pagination cannot sort on PHP-computed virtual accessors in SQL without loading every doctor in the database into memory.
* **Reason for Rejection**: Unacceptable query latency and pagination breakdown. Correlated SQL subqueries in `DoctorPortfolioQueryService` execute in <15ms on SQLite and enable native `ORDER BY` sorting and pagination.

### Alternative D: Materialized Snapshot Roll-up Tables / Background Cron Aggregation
* **Pros**: Pre-aggregates daily metrics into a dedicated summary table (`doctor_roi_snapshots`), simplifying read queries.
* **Cons**:
  1. *Premature Complexity (YAGNI):* The Exponit Labs database currently has 233 doctors and under 1,000 DCR records.
  2. *State Synchronization Lag:* Introduces cache invalidation bugs, delayed updates, and cron job scheduling overhead.
* **Reason for Rejection**: YAGNI. Correlated SQL subqueries on indexed SQLite tables run in under 15ms, eliminating the need for materialized caches in v1.
